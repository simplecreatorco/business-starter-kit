All right, this is the lesson that usually freaks people out, and I get it. So I need you to take a deep breath because this is actually one of the most straightforward lessons in this whole kit.

Let me break this down really simply. Like, let's pretend that you and I are just sitting at a coffee shop. Very casual.

When you start selling things, you need to register your business with your state. The simplest way to do that is as what's called a sole proprietor. This is typically what I recommend everybody start with. It's the fastest and the cheapest option. Most states allow you to register for under $100.

You're basically filing what's called a DBA, which stands for "doing business as." It essentially tells the state, hey, I'm running a business under this name. And that's it. You're official. As a sole proprietor, you and your business are the same legal entity. Your business income goes on your personal tax return. It is simple and easy.

Now at some point down the road, you might want to consider filing as an LLC. LLC stands for limited liability company. An LLC basically creates a legal wall between your personal assets and your business assets. If something ever went wrong and somebody tried to sue your business (which is a very tiny chance, so don't panic), your personal stuff is protected. Your house, your car, your savings.

If you are a person who is a homeowner or has other personal assets, I may consider doing an LLC instead. But if your assets are minimal, sole proprietor all the way for now. The LLC also starts to have some tax benefits as you grow, but that is a conversation to have with a tax professional.

If you do want to go ahead and file as an LLC, it's usually around $300 depending on your state. You can do it yourself online. Google your state, file LLC, and your Secretary of State's website will come up. Most states let you do the entire thing online. It takes like 15 to 20 minutes. One thing I will say: don't do LegalZoom or anything like that. If a platform is trying to charge you to file the LLC for you, skip it. You can totally do it yourself.

Next step. If you file as an LLC, you're going to get what's called an EIN number. E-I-N stands for Employer Identification Number. Think of it like a social security number for your business. You'll use your EIN when you open your business bank account, which we'll talk about in a later lesson.

If you go the sole proprietor route, you will not have an EIN unless you apply for one separately. But you can still open a business bank account as a sole proprietor. You will just use your business name and your social security number instead of your EIN.

Sole Proprietor vs. LLC — The Quick Version

You do not need a law degree for this. Here's the simple breakdown:

Sole Proprietor: The easiest option. You are the business. No paperwork to file (in most states). You can start selling today. The downside? Your personal assets aren't protected if something goes wrong.

LLC: Separates your personal stuff from your business stuff. Costs $50-500 depending on your state. Takes 15-30 minutes to set up online. Recommended once you start making real money.

💡 Rebecca's take: Start as a sole proprietor if you're just getting going. Once you're consistently making money, file for an LLC. Don't let this step keep you from starting.

Getting Your EIN

An EIN (Employer Identification Number) is like a social security number for your business. It's free and takes 5 minutes on the IRS website. You'll need it for taxes and to open a business bank account.

The other big thing: you're going to need a business bank account. It's vital that you keep your business transactions separate from your personal transactions. Written Lesson

Choose Your Work Hours

You started this business for freedom. Don't accidentally build yourself a 24/7 job.

Pick your work hours and write them down. If nap time is your window, great — that's 1-2 hours a day. If it's 8-10 PM after bedtime, that works too. But at your chosen end time, close the laptop. For real.

There will always be one more thing to do. That one more thing will be there tomorrow.

Turn Off Notifications

This one is non-negotiable. Right now, go to your phone settings and turn off Instagram notifications. All of them. Push notifications, DM notifications, like notifications. All of it.

Nothing on Instagram is an emergency. Nothing in your inbox is an emergency. You will check these things during your work hours, and that is enough.

Responding to Emails and DMs

When you're new, every DM feels like it needs an immediate response. It doesn't. Here's your new rule:

Check and respond to DMs and emails once or twice a day, during your work hours. That's it. Nobody is going to stop buying from you because you took 4 hours to reply.

Talk to Your People

If you have a partner, spouse, or family members in your house, have the conversation. Tell them when your work hours are. Tell them what you need during that time. This isn't selfish — it's how businesses survive.

Even a simple "Hey, I'm working from 1-3 during nap time. Can you handle things if one of the kids wakes up?" goes a long way.
